## About osteopathy

### What is an osteopath?

A healthcare professional who specialises in muscle, joint and nerve problems, using hands-on techniques to assess, diagnose and treat. Osteopaths are registered and regulated in the UK, so they must meet set standards of education and practice.

### Who should see an osteopath?

Anyone aged between 0 and 122 years and 164 days! From babies to older adults, people come to us for pain, recovery from injury, or to keep good posture and mobility.

### What does an osteopath treat?

Back pain, neck pain and joint pain, headaches arising from the neck, and sports injuries. Osteopaths look at the whole body when assessing and treating you.

### Is osteopathy safe?

Osteopathy is a safe, non-invasive approach. We take a full history first, explain everything, and use gentle hands-on techniques suited to you.

## Prioritise physical health

Physical health is a complex mix of cardiovascular fitness, stability, mobility, strength, balance and injury prevention. We address each of these with strategies tailored to you, while keeping you safe during activity.

Consistent zone 2 training, for example, has been shown to improve metabolic health by increasing the mitochondria in muscle cells, helping your body take up glucose from the blood for energy and burn fat. This simple activity can help protect against insulin resistance, obesity and type 2 diabetes, and support people already managing metabolic syndrome.

Research shows goal-oriented exercise is among the most effective things you can do for longevity.

## Why we care about sleep

Sleep is often overlooked in conversations about health, which is a mistake given its huge effect on wellbeing. A sleepless night raises blood sugar and cortisol, the stress hormone, and impairs thinking. Research suggests a poorly rested person can be outperformed mentally by someone over the drink-drive limit, with slower reaction times.

Long-term lack of sleep has been linked with neurodegenerative diseases such as dementia. During sleep, the brain clears out waste like amyloid beta and forms new connections, turning what you’ve learned into long-term memory.

Poor sleep affects every system in the body. Most adults need 7 to 8 hours a night, and we’ll help you understand your sleep and find practical ways to improve it, so you wake up refreshed and ready for the day.

## Small actions, big results

Many factors influence how long and how well we live, and each choice we make carries weight. It’s the accumulation of small daily actions that ultimately determines our wellbeing.

## Understanding the types of lower back pain

Knowing which type of pain you have is important for effective treatment and management.

### Acute lower back pain

Comes on suddenly, often after an injury or strain such as heavy lifting, an awkward movement or an accident. It can be intense and make daily activities difficult. Recognising the symptoms early helps you get the right treatment sooner.

### Chronic lower back pain

Lasts more than three months and can come from conditions such as disc degeneration or long-term muscle strain. It’s often a constant dull ache that affects quality of life, so it needs a plan that tackles both the symptoms and the source.

### Referred lower back pain

Felt in the lower back but coming from elsewhere, such as kidney stones, endometriosis or digestive problems. That’s why an accurate diagnosis matters, and we’ll refer you on if your pain isn’t coming from your back.

## Recognising symptoms and getting treatment

Each type of lower back pain has its own symptoms and challenges. Our team is dedicated to giving you a treatment plan tailored to your specific type of pain.

### Osteopathic treatment explained

Our osteopaths assess and treat a wide range of muscle and joint problems, with a particular focus on lower back pain. From palpation and stretching to manipulation and other techniques, each part of treatment is chosen to help you recover and get moving again.

Our aim is to give you the knowledge and confidence to take control of your recovery.

## Techniques we use

We use evidence-based hands-on techniques alongside exercise, and always explain what we’re doing and why.

### Spinal manipulation

A gentle, controlled movement of a spinal joint to reduce stiffness and ease pain, helping you move more freely.

### Soft tissue therapy

Work on muscles, tendons and ligaments, including deep tissue massage and myofascial release, to ease tension and help you relax.

### Joint mobilisation

Passive, rhythmical movements that restore normal joint movement and reduce stiffness, especially helpful for long-standing pain.

### Rehabilitation exercises

A personalised programme to strengthen your core, improve posture and help prevent the problem coming back.

### When to get urgent help

Osteopathy isn’t the right first step if you have:

Numbness around your genitals or bottom

Difficulty passing urine or controlling your bladder or bowels

Weakness in both legs or problems walking

These can be signs of cauda equina syndrome. Go to A&E or call 999 straight away.

Osteoarthritis breaks down the cartilage in the joint, causing pain and stiffness. Other causes include bursitis, tendon problems and fractures.

Hip pain can be aching, stiff or sharp. Your lifestyle, age and previous injuries all play a part, and a thorough assessment helps pinpoint the cause.

### How we treat hip pain

We address both the pain and what’s causing it, using gentle manipulation, stretching and strengthening exercises to restore movement and ease discomfort.

## A guide to hip pain

The hip problems we see most often, and how they affect movement and daily life.

Inflammation of a bursa, a small fluid-filled cushion near the hip, often from repetitive movement or pressure. It typically causes pain on the outside of the hip.

A strain from overdoing it or injury can make walking, running and climbing stairs painful. Rehabilitation helps you recover and prevent it happening again.

Sciatica can cause pain in the hip and buttock that travels down the leg. We assess where it’s coming from and treat the source.

No large meal, alcohol or caffeine for 2–3 hours.

Wear clothes you can squat and move in.

Is the assessment suitable for everyone?

Is this a medical diagnosis?

I'm already fit. Is this for me?

Does it hurt? Are there needles?

No needles and no blood tests. You'll stand on the Tanita, rest your arm on a scanner, lie down for a heart trace, wear a light chest strap, breathe into a spirometer and do a few simple movements on the force plates.

# Stop guessing. Start measuring.

Whether your goal is better health, staying strong for life or finding your extra 1%, you need to know your starting point. Our assessment combines medical, clinical and sports-science grade equipment with clear reporting and education, so you understand how your body is really performing and can take charge of it. Here's what we test, what it means, and why it matters.

Part one

## The six tests

Non-invasive, no needles, around 45 minutes in total.

Medical, clinical & sports-science grade equipment: Tanita MC-780MA · AGE Reader · 12-lead ECG · Frontier X2 · Spirometry · VALD ForceDecks

Body composition

### Tanita Segmental

The Tanita MC-780MA is a clinical-grade, multi-frequency body composition analyser. You stand on it and hold two handles — it takes about 30 seconds.

Your bathroom scalesOne number for your whole body, usually from your feet alone.

Our TanitaEight electrodes and multiple frequencies — measuring each arm, each leg and your trunk separately.

What it measures

Muscle mass — each limbLeft vs right arm and leg, plus trunk. Spots imbalances after injury or from favouring one side.

Fat mass & body fat %Overall, and where it sits in your body.

Visceral fatThe fat stored deep around your organs — the type most linked to health risk.

Body waterTotal water, and the balance inside and outside your cells — linked to hydration, inflammation and recovery.

Phase angleA marker of how healthy your cells are and the quality of your muscle tissue.

Bone mass, BMR & metabolic ageEstimated bone mineral, the calories you burn at rest, and how your metabolism compares with your age.

Why it matters

Two people can weigh exactly the same and be in very different health. Muscle naturally declines from around the age of 30 — a process called sarcopenia — and it protects your metabolism, your joints and your independence later in life. Tracking it, limb by limb, lets us target the right changes, such as strength training and protein intake. For athletes, segmental lean mass and phase angle help track how you are adapting to and recovering from training.

Before your test: avoid a large meal or lots of fluid for 2–3 hours. Not suitable if you have a pacemaker or other implanted electronic device, or are pregnant.

Metabolic health

### Autofluorescence Scan

A painless light scan of your forearm using the AGE Reader, measuring tissue advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) that reflect dietary and metabolic impact.

What it measures

Skin AGE levelHow much has built up, compared with the average for your age.

BodyAge estimateThe age at which an average person has the same level as you.

Why it matters

AGEs build up in your tissues over time and are strongly shaped by diet, blood sugar and lifestyle. A lower score generally reflects healthier ageing. Whole foods, regular exercise and steady blood sugar all help keep them down — supporting your long-term metabolic health.

Before your test: keep your forearms free of creams, sunscreen and fake tan on the day.

Heart

### 12-lead ECG

A resting electrocardiogram that records the electrical activity of your heart while you lie still.

What it measures

Resting heart rateHow many times your heart beats each minute at rest.

Electrical timingsHow quickly the signal travels through your heart and resets between beats (PR, QRS, QTc).

Why it matters

A lower resting heart rate usually reflects better cardiovascular fitness — and cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest predictors of long-term health. This baseline helps us design training that is both safe and effective for you — from gentle Zone 2 work to high-intensity intervals.

Please note: this is a baseline for exercise planning, not a diagnostic heart check. If anything needs additional checking, we'll explain it and advise you of the next step.

Recovery & stress

### HRV with Frontier X2

A lightweight chest strap you wear for around 30 minutes while the rest of your assessment takes place.

What it measures

Heart rate variability (HRV)The tiny changes in time between each heartbeat.

Heart rate responseHow your heart rate rises and settles as you move between tests.

Why it matters

A healthy heart doesn't tick like a metronome. Higher HRV generally means your nervous system is adaptable and recovering well; lower HRV can reflect stress, poor sleep, illness or overtraining. It's a window into recovery for everyone — and a key tool for athletes deciding when to push and when to back off.

Before your test: avoid caffeine and hard exercise beforehand, as both affect HRV.

Lungs

### Spirometry & SpO2

You take a deep breath and blow out as hard and fast as you can into a mouthpiece, repeated a few times for accuracy. We also take a quick reading of your blood oxygen.

What it measures

FVCThe total amount of air you can breathe out.

FEV1How much you can breathe out in the first second.

Lung ageHow your lung function compares with the average for your age.

SpO2Blood oxygen saturation: how well your lungs and circulation deliver oxygen to your body.

Why it matters

Lung function naturally declines with age, and is closely linked to long-term health and how easily you cope with everyday exertion. For athletes, it shows whether breathing could be limiting your endurance. Like every test here, it's a baseline. If anything needs additional checking, we'll advise you of the next step.

Before your test: avoid smoking for at least an hour and a heavy meal for 2 hours. Let us know if you've recently had chest, abdominal or eye surgery, or a recent heart problem.

Power & balance

### Force Plates

Highly sensitive platforms that measure the forces you push into the ground as you stand, squat and jump.

What it measures

Peak powerHow much force you can produce, and how quickly.

Left/right asymmetryHow evenly you share the load between your two legs.

Why it matters

Many people assume losing balance is just part of getting older — but it's often linked to weakness or poor movement patterns that can be trained. Force plates reveal hidden compensations, like leaning on one leg — and alongside your Tanita limb-by-limb results, show whether a weaker side has less muscle, less power, or both — so we can correct them before they lead to joint strain, poor balance or falls. For athletes, the same data shows jump power and left/right differences that can limit performance and are often linked to injury.

What to wear: comfortable clothes and trainers you can squat and move in.

VO2max

Bringing it together

### Your calculated VO2 max

VO2 max is the maximum amount of oxygen your body can use during exercise. It's one of the strongest predictors of long-term health and longevity — and the number longevity experts and athletes care about most.

Measuring it directly means running to complete exhaustion while wearing a mask — not appropriate or necessary for most people. Instead, we calculate yours from the data gathered during your assessment. Because we use the same calculation each time, it's ideal for tracking how your fitness improves.
