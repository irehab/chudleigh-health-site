How to read this page

These results show where you are today. They are compared with the expected range for your age and sex, or with your device's own norms.

They are a starting point for a conversation about your long-term health. They are not a diagnosis, and many of them can change.

This is general education, not personal medical advice. If you ever have chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations, stop and seek medical help.

Where these ranges come from

  • Longevity age: the age your results match. Chudleigh Health Hub turns each result into the age at which an average person of your sex has that result, using the published data listed here. Your longevity age is a weighted average of those ages: fitness counts 3 times (2 when it is estimated), strength twice, and lungs, skin and the Tanita's metabolic age once each. Ages are kept between 20 and 80.
    Who was studied: Not taken from a single study. Each part uses its own reference data. A summary to follow over time, and a starting point for a conversation. It is not a validated measure of biological ageing, and the weights are the clinic's own choice. Fitness counts most because it is one of the strongest signs of long-term health.
  • Fitness age (Nes 2014, the HUNT study). Nes BM, Vatten LJ, Nauman J, Janszky I, Wisloff U. A simple nonexercise model of cardiorespiratory fitness predicts long-term mortality. Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise 2014;46(6):1159-1165. https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000219
    Who was studied: About 37,000 Norwegian adults followed for around 24 years. The idea of a fitness age: the age at which your fitness is average. Here it is worked out from the FRIEND treadmill medians, not from the HUNT tables.
  • Fitness for your age and sex (FRIEND registry 2015). Kaminsky LA, Arena R, Myers J. Reference standards for cardiorespiratory fitness measured with cardiopulmonary exercise testing: data from the Fitness Registry and the Importance of Exercise National Database. Mayo Clinic Proceedings 2015;90(11):1515-1523. Table 3. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4919021/
    Who was studied: 7,783 maximal treadmill tests of US adults aged 20 to 79 without heart disease, with oxygen use measured directly. Your number is an estimate, while these people were measured on a treadmill, so where you sit is a guide. People aged 80 and over are compared with the 70 to 79 group.
  • Grip strength for your age and sex (UK, Dodds 2014). Dodds RM, Syddall HE, Cooper R, et al. Grip strength across the life course: normative data from twelve British studies. PLoS One 2014;9(12):e113637. Table 2. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0113637
    Who was studied: About 50,000 people in Great Britain aged 4 to 90, from 12 studies. Most were measured seated, and the best try was used. The line for low grip (27 kg for men, 16 kg for women) is 2.5 standard deviations below young adult peak strength. The European sarcopenia guidance uses the same line.
  • Lung age (Morris and Temple 1985). Morris JF, Temple W. Spirometric 'lung age' estimation for motivating smoking cessation. Preventive Medicine 1985;14(5):655-662. Lung age from height and FEV1: men 2.87 x height (inches) - 31.25 x FEV1 (litres) - 39.375; women 3.56 x height (inches) - 40 x FEV1 (litres) - 77.28. https://doi.org/10.1016/0091-7435(85)90085-4
    Who was studied: Healthy non-smoking adults in Oregon, USA. A guide to follow over time. A poor-quality breathing test can make the lung age read older than it is.
  • The AGE Reader (Diagnoptics): its own norms, as printed on your report. Printed on the participant's AGE Reader (Diagnoptics) measurement report: the average AGE level for their age, how far above or below it they are, the BodyAge, and the device's own label.
    Who was studied: The device maker's own reference group, which the report does not publish. Used exactly as printed on the report. It is not checked against any other reference data.
  • Tanita MC-780MA: the bands on your report. Printed on the participant's Tanita MC-780MA report: the phase angle marks (7 degrees healthy, 5 'in-risk/ageing', 2 'severely malnourished'), the visceral fat scale (1 to 9 healthy, 10 to 14 high, 15 and above very high), whether each arm and leg's muscle is within Tanita's normal range, and the leg muscle score chart (the line expected for each age).
    Who was studied: The device maker's own reference bands. Used as printed by Tanita. They are not checked against any other reference data.
  • Healthy body fat ranges (Gallagher 2000). Gallagher D, Heymsfield SB, Heo M, Jebb SA, Murgatroyd PR, Sakamoto Y. Healthy percentage body fat ranges: an approach for developing guidelines based on body mass index. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 2000;72(3):694-701. https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/72.3.694
    Who was studied: 1,626 adults with a BMI of 35 or less, measured by laboratory methods. Table 4 (white and African American adults) is used. The authors call these provisional ranges worked out from BMI limits, not definitive ones. Asian adults have a different table. Body fat from a home or clinic bioimpedance scale can differ from the laboratory methods used here.
  • Essential body fat (American Council on Exercise). The American Council on Exercise body fat categories: essential fat is about 2 to 5% for men and 10 to 13% for women, athletes about 6 to 13% for men and 14 to 20% for women. https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/tools-calculators/percent-body-fat-calculator/
    Who was studied: Not a single study. A widely used guide from a fitness education body. Chudleigh Health Hub uses the top of the essential range (5% for men, 13% for women) as its line for very low body fat. Between that line and the healthy range is shown as lean, not as something to work on: the clinic's own working guide.
  • World Health Organization BMI groups. World Health Organization. Obesity: preventing and managing the global epidemic. WHO Technical Report Series 894. Geneva: WHO; 2000. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241208945
    Who was studied: Adults aged 18 and over. BMI is a rough guide. It cannot tell muscle from fat. The WHO table rates the chance of weight-related conditions as average at 18.5 to 24.9, increased at 25 to 29.9 and moderate or higher at 30 and over. It says the risk is graded, begins below 25, and can differ between populations.
  • Left and right muscle balance: the clinic's own guide. Chudleigh Health Hub compares the muscle in your left and right arms, and your left and right legs, from your Tanita report. A difference of 10% or more is marked as room to improve, and 20% or more as a place to focus first.

    Who was studied: An international standard from the European Respiratory Society and the American Thoracic Society. Results are judged by z-scores. -1.645 or above is within the expected range. Between -1.65 and -2.5 is mildly below it, -2.5 to -4 moderately, and under -4 severely.
  • Dutch healthy-adult ECG study (Rijnbeek 2014). Rijnbeek PR, van Herpen G, Bots ML, Man S, Verweij N, Hofman A, Hillege H, Numans ME, Swenne CA, Witteman JCM, Kors JA.
